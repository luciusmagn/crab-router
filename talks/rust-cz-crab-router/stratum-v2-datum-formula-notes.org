#+TITLE: Stratum V2 and DATUM Formula Speaker Notes
#+AUTHOR: Lukáš Hozda
#+OPTIONS: toc:2 num:nil

* General delivery

State what a formula measures before reading its symbols. Then explain the numerator, denominator, and result.

Pronunciations:

- =P_share=: "P sub share", or simply "share payout"
- =D_eff=: "effective share difficulty"
- =D_net=: "network difficulty"
- =R_base=: "block subsidy"
- =F=: "transaction fees"
- =gamma=: "gamma"
- =lambda=: "lambda"
- =min=: "the smaller of"
- =max=: "the larger of"

* Target and difficulty

#+begin_example
hash <= target
#+end_example

Suggested explanation:

"A target is the largest hash value we accept. A lower target is harder to meet. A network target decides whether we found a block; an easier pool target decides whether we found a share."

#+begin_example
D = T1 / target
#+end_example

Suggested explanation:

"Difficulty is the inverse way of expressing a target. When the target becomes smaller, difficulty becomes larger. This is why a larger difficulty number means harder work."

* Difficulty through an aggregating proxy

#+begin_example
D_net > D_up >= D_down
#+end_example

Suggested explanation:

"With one aggregating proxy, a hash can pass three checkpoints. The miner submits hashes meeting the downstream difficulty. The proxy forwards only the smaller subset meeting the pool's upstream difficulty. A still smaller subset meets network difficulty and becomes a block."

If useful, add:

"Higher difficulty means fewer qualifying hashes. The proxy can therefore accept frequent low-difficulty shares from devices while sending a much lower message rate to the pool."

* Actual share difficulty

#+begin_example
D_actual = T1 / hash_value
#+end_example

Suggested explanation:

"Assigned difficulty is the threshold the miner was asked to meet. Actual difficulty describes how good one particular hash happened to be. Pools normally credit the assigned work value, not an extra reward for a share that happened to exceed it by a large margin."

* Coinbase construction

#+begin_example
coinbase = coinb1 || extranonce1 || extranonce2 || coinb2
#+end_example

Suggested explanation:

"The pool sends the bytes before and after the mutable extranonce area. Extranonce1 identifies the connection. The miner changes extranonce2. Concatenating all four parts produces the serialized coinbase transaction. Changing extranonce2 changes the coinbase transaction ID, the Merkle root, and therefore the block header."

The double bar means byte concatenation, not arithmetic addition.

* Version rolling

#+begin_example
negotiated_mask = miner_mask & server_mask
#+end_example

Suggested explanation:

"The miner advertises the bits it can roll, and the server advertises the bits it permits. The bitwise AND keeps only bits allowed by both sides."

#+begin_example
nVersion = (job_version & ~mask) | (version_bits & mask)
#+end_example

Suggested explanation:

"First preserve every job-version bit outside the mask. Then insert the miner-selected values only inside the mask. The miner cannot change signalling or policy bits excluded by the server."

* Basic FPPS payout

#+begin_example
P_share = (D_eff / D_net) * (R_base + F)
#+end_example

Suggested explanation:

"This formula pays one share its expected fraction of a block. The difficulty ratio tells us what fraction of the expected work for one network block this share represents. We multiply that fraction by the total block value: the subsidy plus transaction fees."

Then define the terms:

- =P_share= is the gross payout credited for one accepted share.
- =D_eff= is the difficulty credited to that share.
- =D_net= is the current Bitcoin network difficulty.
- =R_base= is the block subsidy. It does not include fees.
- =F= is the transaction-fee value assigned to the job.

A useful short version is:

"Expected block fraction, multiplied by expected block reward."

** Numerical example

#+begin_example
D_eff  = 1,000,000
D_net  = 100,000,000,000,000
R_base = 3.125 BTC
F      = 0.250 BTC
#+end_example

Suggested explanation:

"The share represents one hundred-millionth of the expected work for a block. The attempted block is worth 3.375 bitcoin. Multiplying those values gives 3.375 satoshis of gross expected payout for the share."

The result is before pool fees. Pools can maintain fractional-satoshi account balances.

* Full-template FPPS

#+begin_example
P_share = (D_eff / D_net) * (R_base + F_verified)
#+end_example

Suggested explanation:

"This is the ordinary FPPS calculation, except the fee value is explicitly verified. The miner selected the transactions, but the JDS received the transaction list, obtained anything missing, validated the template, and calculated its fees. The pool does not need to construct the template in order to verify its value."

Main point:

"Miner selection and pool validation are separate operations."

Because =F_verified= is calculated from the full transaction set, no fee cap is needed for this mode.

* Coinbase-only fee cap

#+begin_example
F_cap = min(F_declared, gamma * F_avg)
#+end_example

Suggested explanation:

"In coinbase-only mode the pool cannot inspect the transactions. It therefore pays the smaller of two values: the fees claimed by the miner, or a policy cap based on recent network fees."

Definitions:

- =F_declared= is the miner's claimed fee amount.
- =F_avg= is the pool's recent average or reference fee amount.
- =gamma= is a configurable multiplier applied to that average.
- =F_cap= is the fee value actually used for per-share payment.

** Gamma example

#+begin_example
F_avg = 0.200 BTC
gamma = 1.4

gamma * F_avg = 0.280 BTC
#+end_example

Suggested explanation:

"Gamma is just the cap multiplier. With gamma set to 1.4, the pool is willing to credit at most 140 percent of its recent average fee value. Here that limit is 0.28 bitcoin."

Do not describe gamma as proving the fee claim. It only limits pool exposure.

* Coinbase-only FPPS payout

#+begin_example
P_share = (D_eff / D_net) * (R_base + F_cap)
#+end_example

Suggested explanation:

"The share calculation is unchanged, but the unverifiable fee claim is replaced by the capped fee value. The pool pays full expected subsidy and only the fee amount permitted by its risk policy."

* Surplus bonus

#+begin_example
B_surplus = lambda * max(0, F_real - F_cap)
#+end_example

Suggested explanation:

"If the miner finds a valid block, the pool can finally inspect its real fees. We subtract the amount already covered by the cap. The max operation prevents a negative bonus. Lambda then selects what fraction of the remaining surplus is paid to the miner."

Read the expression in three steps:

1. Calculate =F_real - F_cap=.
2. Replace a negative result with zero.
3. Multiply the result by =lambda=.

Definitions:

- =F_real= is the actual fee value in the valid block.
- =F_cap= is the fee value already included in per-share FPPS.
- =lambda= is a value from zero to one.
- =B_surplus= is a one-off bonus for the found block, not a payment on every share.

** Lambda example

#+begin_example
F_real = 0.400 BTC
F_cap  = 0.280 BTC
lambda = 0.8

B_surplus = 0.8 * max(0, 0.400 - 0.280)
          = 0.096 BTC
#+end_example

Suggested explanation:

"The block contains 0.12 bitcoin more fees than the cap covered. Lambda is 0.8, so the miner receives 80 percent of that surplus: 0.096 bitcoin."

If actual fees are below the cap, =max(0, ...)= makes the bonus zero.

* Closing FPPS explanation

A concise summary for the audience:

"Full-template mode lets the pool verify fees before paying shares, so standard FPPS works directly. Coinbase-only mode hides the transactions, so the pool needs a fee cap or another risk policy. The limitation comes from missing information, not from miner-created templates themselves."
